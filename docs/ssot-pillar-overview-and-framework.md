Pillar Overview: EECA Lung Health Sovereignty Hub
Three Pillars for Political Certainty, Operational Continuity, and Accountability
Lung health represents a critical intersection of infectious disease, non-communicable disease, and health security. It is highly sensitive to financing gaps, system disruptions, and policy fragmentation, making it a strategic entry point for strengthening broader health system resilience.
​​
The EECA Lung Health Sovereignty Hub is structured around three mutually reinforcing pillars. Together, they enable governments, parliaments, and national stakeholders to strengthen financing decisions, improve operational coordination, and ensure accountability for implementation.
Primary Users
The Hub is designed for three core user groups:
Ministries of Health and Finance (budgeting, planning, resource allocation)
Parliamentarians and oversight bodies (debate, approval, accountability)
Technical and implementing partners (analysis, coordination, delivery)

Summary Table
Pillar
Core Purpose
Main Deliverables
Decision Value
Pillar 1. Budget and Policy Intelligence
Improve financing and policy decision-making
Budget review reports, resource mapping, policy costing models, spending alignment recommendations, legislative and budget impact notes
Helps governments understand where money is going, where gaps and inefficiencies exist, and which policy choices are financially realistic
Pillar 2. Operational Intelligence and Health Security
Strengthen planning, forecasting, and continuity of care
Interactive dashboards, burden vs spending analyses, forecasting models, decision briefs, health security coordination protocols, data governance framework
Helps decision-makers track system performance, anticipate risks, and coordinate responses during routine operations and disruption
Pillar 3. Parliamentary and Public Accountability
Ensure decisions are debated, tracked, and followed through
Caucus engagement frameworks, budget advocacy toolkits, oversight and monitoring frameworks, accountability scorecards, structured dialogue mechanisms
Helps convert evidence into political ownership, implementation follow-through, and stronger transparency




Budget and Policy Intelligence
Purpose
Support ministries, parliaments, and national stakeholders to align lung health financing and policy decisions with disease burden, system gaps, and fiscal constraints.
Core Deliverables
Budget Review Reports (pilot countries or selected contexts)
Resource Mapping Analyses (funding flows, overlaps, gaps)
Policy Costing Models (selected interventions or reforms)
Spending Alignment Recommendations (linked to burden and priorities)
Legislative and Budget Impact Notes (selected proposals or amendments)


What this pillar enables
Clear visibility on current allocation of lung health resources
Identification of inefficiencies and underfunded priorities
Credible costing of policy and legislative options
Informed financing decisions in the context of donor transition


Strategic role within the Hub
This pillar provides the financial and policy intelligence required to move from commitment to evidence-based allocation and reform.

Pillar 2 - Operational Intelligence and Health Security
Purpose
Provide structured evidence and operational insight to support planning, procurement, service continuity, and crisis response.
Core Deliverables
Interactive Dashboards (burden, spending, coverage, operational indicators)
Burden vs Spending Analysis Reports
Forecasting Models (procurement, budget scenarios, service demand)
Decision Briefs (actionable policy and operational options)
Health Security Coordination Protocols (selected disruption scenarios)
Data Governance and Indicator Framework (sources, validation, access rules)


What this pillar enables
System-wide visibility of performance and resource allocation
Early identification of operational and financing risks
Integration of forecasting into decision-making processes
Continuity of lung health services during disruption


Strategic role within the Hub
This pillar functions as the operational intelligence layer, transforming fragmented data into structured insight for both routine decision-making and coordinated crisis response. AI is applied as an enabling analytical tool, not as the core dependency.



Pillar 3 - Parliamentary and Public Accountability
Purpose
Ensure that financing and policy decisions are debated, monitored, and followed through through structured parliamentary engagement and accountability mechanisms.
Core Deliverables
Caucus Engagement Frameworks (participating countries)
Budget Advocacy Toolkits (parliamentarians and civil society)
Oversight and Monitoring Frameworks (allocation → implementation tracking)
Accountability Scorecards / Tracking Tools
Structured Dialogue Mechanisms (parliament, civil society, technical actors)
Community-informed feedback channels (context-dependent)


What this pillar enables
Evidence-based parliamentary and policy debate
Tracking of decisions from allocation to implementation
Increased transparency in resource use
Strengthened accountability between political actors, civil society, and technical stakeholders


Strategic role within the Hub
This pillar ensures that analysis translates into accountable political action, closing the gap between decision-making and implementation.



Cross-Cutting Function - Governance and Regional Coordination
Purpose
Provide strategic oversight, ensure alignment across pillars, and maintain regional coordination and continuity under both routine and crisis conditions.
Core Functions
Regional Steering Structure (operational decision-making body)
Advisory and community input mechanism
Regional partner coordination (ministries, civil society, technical actors)
Quarterly delivery and performance reviews
Health security coordination across disruption scenarios
Sustainability and funding strategy
Governance model for expansion


Role
This function integrates the three pillars into a single operating mechanism, ensuring that financing intelligence, operational insight, and accountability processes reinforce each other and remain aligned with national and regional priorities.


Implementation Roadmap 

Purpose
The Implementation Roadmap defines how the EECA Lung Health Sovereignty Hub moves from concept to operational reality within a 12-month horizon.
It serves three core functions:
Translate strategy into execution - Convert the three pillars into concrete, time-bound actions, outputs, and responsibilities.


Provide political and operational certainty - Give governments and partners a clear view of what will be delivered, when, and with what level of effort and investment.


Enable accountability and tracking - Establish milestones and deliverables that can be monitored, evaluated, and adjusted as implementation progresses.


Phases Overview
0–3 months: Foundation + diagnostics
3–6 months: Initial tools + pilot outputs
6–9 months: Operational use + decision support
9–12 months: Institutionalization + scale readiness


0–3 Months - Foundation and Diagnostics
Objective
 Establish baseline, define scope, and align stakeholders.
Pillar 1
Rapid budget landscape scans (pilot countries)
Resource mapping (funding flows, gaps, overlaps)
Stakeholder mapping (MoH, MoF, Parliament, donors)


Pillar 2
Core indicator framework defined
Data source inventory completed
Dashboard architecture and analytical workflows designed
Initial burden vs spending baseline analysis


Pillar 3
Political economy and decision-making pathway analysis
Mapping of parliamentary and civil society actors
Draft engagement and accountability approach


Governance
Steering structure operationalized
Pilot countries confirmed
Partner network mapped
Initial risk and disruption scenarios identified



3–6 Months - Initial Tools and Pilot Outputs
Objective
 Deliver first usable outputs and test in pilot countries.
Pillar 1
Budget Review Reports (1–2 pilot countries)
Initial Policy Costing Models (selected interventions)
Draft Spending Alignment Recommendations


Pillar 2
Beta dashboards launched
Burden vs Spending Analysis Reports (country level)
Initial forecasting models developed (e.g. procurement)
First decision briefs produced


Pillar 3
Parliamentary engagement initiated in pilot countries
Budget advocacy toolkit developed
Initial accountability tracking approach tested


Governance
First quarterly review completed
Draft health security coordination protocol
Initial sustainability discussions initiated



6–9 Months - Operationalization and Decision Support
Objective
 Move from tools to real decision use.
Pillar 1
Refined policy costing linked to active policy discussions
Legislative and budget impact notes used in decision processes
Updated resource allocation recommendations
Pillar 2
Dashboards expanded (multi-country view)
Forecasting models applied to real decisions
Regular decision briefs issued
Health security coordination workflows tested
Pillar 3
Caucus engagement frameworks formalized
Structured advocacy cycles aligned with budget timelines
Oversight tools introduced (tracking, hearings, scorecards)
Governance
Regional coordination processes stabilized
Mid-term performance and learning review completed



9–12 Months - Institutionalization and Scale Readiness
Objective
 Embed functions and prepare for expansion.
Pillar 1
Comparative budget and policy analysis across pilot countries
Policy costing approach documented for reuse
Pathways for institutional adoption defined
Pillar 2
Dashboards and analytics standardized
Regional evidence synthesis produced
Health security coordination mechanism documented
Pillar 3
Accountability frameworks institutionalized (where feasible)
Monitoring cycles established
Engagement model documented
Governance
Final MVP review completed
Sustainability and scale roadmap delivered
Governance model formalized

Governance, Risk & Sustainability Model
Purpose
This document defines the governance structure, risk management approach, and sustainability pathway for the EECA Lung Health Sovereignty Hub during its 12-month MVP phase.
The model is designed to ensure that the Hub remains:
regionally grounded
politically credible
operationally manageable
accountable to partners and funders
scalable beyond the MVP period


The governance approach is intentionally lean. It is designed to support decision-making, coordination, and delivery without creating unnecessary institutional complexity.

Core Team
The EECA Lung Health Sovereignty Hub is led by a multidisciplinary team combining health systems expertise, policy design, and advanced data and AI system development.

Gayané Arustamyan
Director | Health Information Systems Specialist
A senior health and human rights expert with 15+ years of experience in HIV program design, grant management, and monitoring & evaluation across multiple regions.
Gayané brings extensive experience with the Global Fund and other major donors, having led large-scale, multi-country health initiatives and a strong track record in aligning donor funding, national programs, and data systems to improve outcomes and accountability.
Role in the Hub Leads overall program direction, health systems integration, and alignment with national and regional stakeholders.

Alesia Matusevich
Lead Coordinator | Policy & Legal Frameworks
A global health advocate with over a decade of experience at the intersection of legal reform and public health systems.
Alesia specializes in designing policy and regulatory frameworks that enable sustainable and rights-based healthcare delivery.
Role in the Hub: Leads policy design, legislative alignment, and integration of governance mechanisms into financing and accountability structures.

Daniel Fernandes
AI & Systems Architecture Lead | Operational Intelligence
Daniel Fernandes leads the design of the Hub’s operational architecture, translating complex policy, financing, and service-delivery needs into a functional system for decision-makers. 
He structures how the Hub works across its pillars - organizing information, generating intelligence, and ensuring outputs are clear, actionable, scalable, and implementable.
Role in the Hub: Leads the design of decision-support systems, advanced analytics and decision-support methods (AI-enabled), and the overall architecture connecting the three pillars into a functional, deployable system.
Governance Structure
The Hub operates through a lean governance model designed to enable fast decision-making while ensuring accountability.
Core Team (Execution & Design)
Responsible for day-to-day implementation, delivery of outputs, and coordination with partners.
Advisory Layer (Strategic Guidance)
A small group of external experts and regional stakeholders providing periodic input on:
policy alignment
regional relevance
technical validity
Country Engagement (Optional / Pilot-based)
 In pilot countries, focal points or partner institutions support:
data validation
policy contextualization
stakeholder access



Decision-Making Model
Strategic decisions (scope, partnerships, funding priorities)
 Led by Director, with input from Core Team
Technical and system design decisions
 Led by AI & Systems Architecture Lead
Policy and governance decisions
 Led by Lead Coordinator
Operational decisions (delivery, timelines, outputs)
 Shared across Core Team, coordinated by Director




Risk Management Approach
The Hub adopts a pragmatic, early-identification approach to risk, focusing on operational, political, and technical constraints.
Key Risks & Mitigation
Data availability and quality limitations
 → Use proxy indicators, triangulation, and phased data validation


Limited political uptake or engagement
 → Produce early, tangible outputs (e.g. budget briefs) to demonstrate value


Over-complexity of tools or systems
 → Maintain a “minimum viable intelligence” approach; prioritize usability over sophistication


Coordination challenges across countries and stakeholders
 → Focus initial implementation on a limited number of pilot contexts


Dependency on external partners or data sources
 → Design systems to function with partial data and adaptable inputs


Sustainability Pathway
The Hub is designed to transition from a funded MVP into a sustainable, embedded regional mechanism.
During the 12-month MVP:
Deliver high-value, decision-relevant outputs
Demonstrate utility to governments and partners
Build trust through practical application


Post-MVP pathways:
Integration into regional institutions or platforms
Co-funding by governments and international partners
Expansion into additional disease areas or health domains
Development of a modular service model (policy support, analytics, monitoring tools)

# Pillar Overview: EECA Lung Health Sovereignty Hub

## Three Pillars for Political Certainty, Operational Continuity, and Accountability

Lung health represents a critical intersection of infectious disease, non-communicable disease, and health security. It is highly sensitive to financing gaps, system disruptions, and policy fragmentation, making it a strategic entry point for strengthening broader health system resilience.

The EECA Lung Health Sovereignty Hub is structured around three mutually reinforcing pillars. Together, they enable governments, parliaments, and national stakeholders to strengthen financing decisions, improve operational coordination, and ensure accountability for implementation.

---

## Primary Users

The Hub is designed for three core user groups:

- **Ministries of Health and Finance** (budgeting, planning, resource allocation)
- **Parliamentarians and oversight bodies** (debate, approval, accountability)
- **Technical and implementing partners** (analysis, coordination, delivery)

Budget and Policy Intelligence** | Improve financing and policy decision-making | Budget review reports, resource mapping, policy costing models, spending alignment recommendations, legislative and budget impact notes | Helps governments understand where money is going, where gaps and inefficiencies exist, and which policy choices are financially realistic |

Operational Intelligence and Health Security** | Strengthen planning, forecasting, and continuity of care | Interactive dashboards, burden vs spending analyses, forecasting models, decision briefs, health security coordination protocols, data governance framework | Helps decision-makers track system performance, anticipate risks, and coordinate responses during routine operations and disruption |
| **Pillar 3. # Pillar 1 – Budget and Policy Intelligence

## Purpose

Support ministries, parliaments, and national stakeholders to align lung health financing and policy decisions with disease burden, system gaps, and fiscal constraints.

## Core Deliverables

- Budget Review Reports (pilot countries or selected contexts)
- Resource Mapping Analyses (funding flows, overlaps, gaps)
- Policy Costing Models (selected interventions or reforms)
- Spending Alignment Recommendations (linked to burden and priorities)
- Legislative and Budget Impact Notes (selected proposals or amendments)

## What this pillar enables

- Clear visibility on current allocation of lung health resources
- Identification of inefficiencies and underfunded priorities
- Credible costing of policy and legislative options
- Informed financing decisions in the context of donor transition

## Strategic role within the Hub

This pillar provides the financial and policy intelligence required to move from commitment to evidence-based allocation and reform.

---

# Pillar 2 – Operational Intelligence and Health Security

## Purpose

Provide structured evidence and operational insight to support planning, procurement, service continuity, and crisis response.

## Core Deliverables

- Interactive Dashboards (burden, spending, coverage, operational indicators)
- Burden vs Spending Analysis Reports
- Forecasting Models (procurement, budget scenarios, service demand)
- Decision Briefs (actionable policy and operational options)
- Health Security Coordination Protocols (selected disruption scenarios)
- Data Governance and Indicator Framework (sources, validation, access rules)

## What this pillar enables

- System-wide visibility of performance and resource allocation
- Early identification of operational and financing risks
- Integration of forecasting into decision-making processes
- Continuity of lung health services during disruption

## Strategic role within the Hub

This pillar functions as the operational intelligence layer, transforming fragmented data into structured insight for both routine decision-making and coordinated crisis response. **AI is applied as an enabling analytical tool, not as the core dependency.**

---

# Pillar 3 – Parliamentary and Public Accountability

## Purpose

Ensure that financing and policy decisions are debated, monitored, and followed through through structured parliamentary engagement and accountability mechanisms.

## Core Deliverables

- Caucus Engagement Frameworks (participating countries)
- Budget Advocacy Toolkits (parliamentarians and civil society)
- Oversight and Monitoring Frameworks (allocation → implementation tracking)
- Accountability Scorecards / Tracking Tools
- Structured Dialogue Mechanisms (parliament, civil society, technical actors)
- Community-informed feedback channels (context-dependent)

## What this pillar enables

- Evidence-based parliamentary and policy debate
- Tracking of decisions from allocation to implementation
- Increased transparency in resource use
- Strengthened accountability between political actors, civil society, and technical stakeholders

## Strategic role within the Hub

This pillar ensures that analysis translates into accountable political action, closing the gap between decision-making and implementation.

Provide strategic oversight, ensure alignment across pillars, and maintain regional coordination and continuity under both routine and crisis conditions.

# Implementation Roadmap

## Purpose

The Implementation Roadmap defines how the EECA Lung Health Sovereignty Hub moves from concept to operational reality within a 12-month horizon.

It serves three core functions:

1. **Translate strategy into execution** – Convert the three pillars into concrete, time-bound actions, outputs, and responsibilities.
2. **Provide political and operational certainty** – Give governments and partners a clear view of what will be delivered, when, and with what level of effort and investment.
3. **Enable accountability and tracking** – Establish milestones and deliverables that can be monitored, evaluated, and adjusted as implementation progresses.

## Phases Overview

- **0–3 months:** Foundation + diagnostics
- **3–6 months:** Initial tools + pilot outputs
- **6–9 months:** Operational use + decision support
- **9–12 months:** Institutionalization + scale readiness

---

## 0–3 Months – Foundation and Diagnostics

**Objective:** Establish baseline, define scope, and align stakeholders.

| Pillar | Activities |
|--------|------------|
| **Pillar 1** | Rapid budget landscape scans (pilot countries); Resource mapping (funding flows, gaps, overlaps); Stakeholder mapping (MoH, MoF, Parliament, donors) |
| **Pillar 2** | Core indicator framework defined; Data source inventory completed; Dashboard architecture and analytical workflows designed; Initial burden vs spending baseline analysis |
| **Pillar 3** | Political economy and decision-making pathway analysis; Mapping of parliamentary and civil society actors; Draft engagement and accountability approach |
| **Governance** | Steering structure operationalized; Pilot countries confirmed; Partner network mapped; Initial risk and disruption scenarios identified |

---

## 3–6 Months – Initial Tools and Pilot Outputs

**Objective:** Deliver first usable outputs and test in pilot countries.

| Pillar | Activities |
|--------|------------|
| **Pillar 1** | Budget Review Reports (1–2 pilot countries); Initial Policy Costing Models (selected interventions); Draft Spending Alignment Recommendations |
| **Pillar 2** | Beta dashboards launched; Burden vs Spending Analysis Reports (country level); Initial forecasting models developed (e.g. procurement); First decision briefs produced |
| **Pillar 3** | Parliamentary engagement initiated in pilot countries; Budget advocacy toolkit developed; Initial accountability tracking approach tested |
| **Governance** | First quarterly review completed; Draft health security coordination protocol; Initial sustainability discussions initiated |

---

## 6–9 Months – Operationalization and Decision Support

**Objective:** Move from tools to real decision use.

| Pillar | Activities |
|--------|------------|
| **Pillar 1** | Refined policy costing linked to active policy discussions; Legislative and budget impact notes used in decision processes; Updated resource allocation recommendations |
| **Pillar 2** | Dashboards expanded (multi-country view); Forecasting models applied to real decisions; Regular decision briefs issued; Health security coordination workflows tested |
| **Pillar 3** | Caucus engagement frameworks formalized; Structured advocacy cycles aligned with budget timelines; Oversight tools introduced (tracking, hearings, scorecards) |
| **Governance** | Regional coordination processes stabilized; Mid-term performance and learning review completed |

---

## 9–12 Months – Institutionalization and Scale Readiness

**Objective:** Embed functions and prepare for expansion.

| Pillar | Activities |
|--------|------------|
| **Pillar 1** | Comparative budget and policy analysis across pilot countries; Policy costing approach documented for reuse; Pathways for institutional adoption defined |
| **Pillar 2** | Dashboards and analytics standardized; Regional evidence synthesis produced; Health security coordination mechanism documented |
| **Pillar 3** | Accountability frameworks institutionalized (where feasible); Monitoring cycles established; Engagement model documented |
| **Governance** | Final MVP review completed; Sustainability and scale roadmap delivered; Governance model formalized |

# Governance, Risk & Sustainability Model

## Purpose

This document defines the governance structure, risk management approach, and sustainability pathway for the EECA Lung Health Sovereignty Hub during its 12-month MVP phase.

The model is designed to ensure that the Hub remains:

- regionally grounded
- politically credible
- operationally manageable
- accountable to partners and funders

The governance approach is intentionally lean. It is designed to support decision-making, coordination, and delivery without creating unnecessary institutional complexity.

## Risk Management Approach

The Hub adopts a pragmatic, early-identification approach to risk, focusing on operational, political, and technical constraints.

| Key Risks | Mitigation |
|-----------|------------|
| Data availability and quality limitations | Use proxy indicators, triangulation, and phased data validation |
| Limited political uptake or engagement | Produce early, tangible outputs (e.g. budget briefs) to demonstrate value |
| Over-complexity of tools or systems | Maintain a "minimum viable intelligence" approach; prioritize usability over sophistication |
| Coordination challenges across countries and stakeholders | Focus initial implementation on a limited number of pilot contexts |
| Dependency on external partners or data sources | Design systems to function with partial data and adaptable inputs |
